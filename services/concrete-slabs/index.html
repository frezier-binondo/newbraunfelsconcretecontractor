Concrete Slab Contractor in New Braunfels, TX

Garage floors, patio bases, shop slabs, and equipment pads built with solid preparation and reinforcement.

Concrete slabs form the working surface for garages, workshops, storage buildings, outdoor living bases, and light equipment pads. In New Braunfels they must perform under vehicle loads, tool traffic, temperature swings, and occasional moisture. A well-prepared slab starts with the subgrade and ends with controlled finishing and curing—not just the pour itself.

Residential Concrete Slabs

Common residential applications include garage floors, patio or outdoor living bases, garden shed pads, and utility slabs. We evaluate access, existing grade, drainage, and how the slab will be used so thickness, reinforcement, and joint layout match the expected loads. Garage slabs, for example, need to support vehicles and resist oil and moisture; outdoor slabs need to drain and resist sun exposure.

Shop and Equipment Slabs

Workshops, metal buildings, and equipment pads often require thicker sections or additional reinforcement depending on the loads. We discuss intended use—vehicle storage, heavy tools, or stationary equipment—during planning. We do not provide structural engineering or guaranteed load ratings; designs that require engineering should follow applicable building codes and qualified professional input.

Site Preparation and Reinforcement

Successful slabs depend on what is underneath. Unsuitable soil is removed or stabilized as needed. A compacted aggregate base provides a uniform support layer. Forms establish elevation and edge control. Reinforcement (mesh or rebar) is placed to help control cracking under load. Drainage is planned so water does not pond on or under the slab.

In the New Braunfels, soils can vary and rock may be present. Site conditions are assessed so the base and thickness are appropriate for the specific property rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

Placement, Finishing, and Curing

Concrete is placed, leveled, and finished to the required flatness and texture. Control joints are cut or formed at planned intervals. Curing is especially important in hot New Braunfels weather; moisture retention during the early strength-gain period helps the slab develop properly. We provide guidance on when the surface can be used and any recommended protection during the first weeks.

Related Services
